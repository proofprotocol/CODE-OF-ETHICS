# Proof Economy Code of Ethics

**Foundational Ethical and Institutional Obligations for Proof Economy Participation**

**Status:** Foundational Governance Document  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Issued by:** HACKERverse® / Nebulonium, Inc.  
**Author:** Craig Ellrod  
**Governance:** Proof Economy Standards Alliance (PESA)

> **Proof is not only a technical result. Proof is a relationship between a claim, evidence, a producer, a verifier, and a consumer who must decide whether reliance is justified.**

---

## 1. Purpose

The Proof Economy requires more than technical interoperability. Independently verifiable evidence depends not only on cryptography, provenance, witnessing, and reproducibility, but also on the conduct of the people and institutions that produce, verify, certify, govern, finance, or materially influence proof.

This Code establishes the foundational ethical obligations for **recognized participation, certification, governance, and commercial activity within the Proof Economy**.

Merely reading, discussing, implementing, or referencing a public Proof Protocol specification does not by itself create a contractual obligation under this Code. Where compliance with this Code is required for recognition, certification, membership, licensing, accreditation, procurement, or another commercial relationship, that obligation SHOULD be established explicitly through the applicable agreement or participation terms.

---

## 2. Foundational Principle

A root of trust is not proof of trustworthiness.

Cryptographic authenticity may establish identity, provenance, integrity, or temporal facts. It does not establish that the people or organizations controlling those mechanisms have acted ethically, independently, competently, or transparently.

**Obfuscation is not independence. Anonymity is not integrity. Cryptographic authenticity is not ethical legitimacy.**

No producer, verifier, standards body, foundation, certification authority, registry, financial participant, or governing institution is exempt from the standards of evidence, integrity, transparency, and accountability it expects others to satisfy.

---

## 3. Scope

This Code may apply, through explicit recognition or participation requirements, to:

- Independent individuals and representatives;
- Proof producers and verifiers;
- Standards bodies, foundations, and governance organizations;
- Technology vendors and benchmark operators;
- Certification authorities and registries;
- Investors, family offices, banks, lenders, insurers, asset managers, private equity, venture capital, and other financial interests;
- Auditors, advisers, consultants, intermediaries, and agents; and
- Any person or institution exercising direct or indirect material influence over proof-related decisions.

The obligation follows the relevant person, institution, transaction, and ultimate controlling interest wherever material influence is exercised.

---

## 4. Trusted Baseline

Trust MUST be supported by documented controls and evidence rather than institutional reputation or self-attestation.

Recognized participants SHOULD be assessable against the following dimensions:

| Requirement | Fundamental question |
|---|---|
| Identity | Who is responsible for the proof and its governance? |
| Background verification | Have relevant qualifications, histories, and material integrity concerns been appropriately checked? |
| Beneficial ownership | Who owns, finances, or ultimately controls the institution? |
| Independence | Are material relationships and conflicts disclosed and managed? |
| Ethical conduct | What enforceable standards govern participants? |
| Competence | Are responsible people qualified for their roles? |
| Governance | Who appoints leaders and exercises decision-making authority? |
| Auditability | Can independent reviewers inspect relevant decisions, activities, and controls? |
| Accountability | Can misconduct be investigated, challenged, and sanctioned? |
| Continuous assurance | Are controls reassessed as people, interests, and circumstances change? |
| Ethical business practices | Do commercial conduct, contractual dealings, financial disclosures, intellectual-property practices, and treatment of stakeholders meet documented, independently assessable standards? |

Background verification MUST be lawful, proportionate, relevant to the responsibilities involved, and conducted with appropriate privacy and due-process protections. It is an input to risk assessment, not a guarantee of ethical behavior.

---

## 5. Standards Bodies and Proof Stewards

An institution that establishes standards for others SHOULD demonstrate how it meets equivalent expectations in its own governance and operations.

Relevant records SHOULD include, as appropriate:

- Leadership and governance;
- Funding and beneficial ownership;
- Conflicts of interest;
- Standards-development procedures;
- Material decisions;
- Certification and registry policies; and
- Corrective actions.

No institution SHOULD be the sole, unchallengeable authority certifying its own trustworthiness.

When public anonymity is justified, responsible people MUST still be identifiable to appropriately authorized independent oversight. Legitimate confidentiality, privacy, and security safeguards MUST NOT become immunity from accountability.

---

## 6. Separation of Standards and Certification

Standards development and certification are distinct functions and MUST be governed so that commercial, institutional, or technical interests cannot substitute administrative approval for independently examinable proof.

A party with a material interest in a conformance outcome MUST disclose that interest and MUST NOT exercise undisclosed or inappropriate control over the requirements, evidence evaluation, or certification decision applicable to that outcome.

### 6.1 Evidence-Based Certification

**Certification MUST be supported by independently examinable evidence and MUST meet the requirements of a valid proof under the applicable Proof Protocol specification. Questionnaires, presentations, badges, gateways, self-attestations, declarations, administrative approvals, or similar mechanisms MUST NOT, by themselves, constitute proof of conformance or a sufficient basis for certification.**

A certification determination MUST identify the applicable specification, the evidence supporting the determination, the scope of the claim, and the proof record upon which the determination relies.

### 6.2 Gateways Are Not Proof

A **gateway** is an administrative, procedural, commercial, membership, payment, or access-control step used to authorize participation or progression.

**Passing a gateway does not establish technical efficacy, proof validity, or conformance unless the underlying determination is supported by the evidence required by the applicable specification and meets the requirements of a valid proof.**

A gateway MAY control access to a program, process, registry, evaluation, or certification workflow. It MUST NOT be represented as technical proof merely because the participant successfully passed the gateway.

### 6.3 No Self-Certification by Renaming

A questionnaire, declaration, presentation, document review, badge, gateway, membership decision, administrative approval, or vendor-controlled assessment does not become independent proof merely by being labeled verification, validation, assurance, certification, accreditation, or conformance.

Where independent certification is claimed, the evidence and proof determination MUST be independently examinable and the relevant conflicts, roles, authority, and scope MUST be disclosed.

### 6.4 Governance of Certification

Organizations responsible for standards governance SHOULD maintain structural and procedural safeguards between standards development and certification sufficient to protect independent judgment and prevent standards capture.

Certification criteria, evidence requirements, material decision procedures, conflicts, recusals, and challenge mechanisms SHOULD be documented and auditable.

## 7. Ethical Business Practices

Technically valid evidence does not excuse unethical commercial conduct.

Recognized Proof Economy participants SHOULD maintain documented and assessable practices addressing:

- Truthful marketing and representations;
- Fair contracting and licensing;
- Respect for intellectual-property rights;
- Transparent material financial relationships and beneficial ownership;
- Anti-bribery and anti-corruption controls;
- Responsible treatment of employees, contributors, partners, customers, and counterparties;
- Related-party transactions and conflicts;
- Reporting and investigation of misconduct; and
- Appropriate corrective action.

Allegations MUST be assessed through documented, impartial procedures that protect good-faith complainants, provide fair process, and distinguish substantiated findings from unverified claims.

## 8. Trusted Capital Standard

Capital is not proof of credibility. Financial power is not proof of integrity.

The Trusted Capital Standard extends the Trusted Baseline to institutions and individuals that finance, own, control, acquire, insure, advise, or exercise material influence over Proof Economy participants.

It encompasses financial institutions and interests including banks, venture capital and private equity firms, family offices, hedge funds, asset managers, investment banks, institutional investors, lenders, insurers, financial intermediaries, independent investors, advisers, brokers, agents, trustees, beneficial owners, board members, and representatives.

### 7.1 Financial Integrity Requirements

| Dimension | Required evidence or practice |
|---|---|
| Ownership and control | Verify relevant beneficial ownership, controlling interests, and decision-making authority. |
| Source of funds | Provide appropriately scoped evidence of lawful funding and applicable compliance. |
| Financial reporting | Maintain accurate, auditable records and truthful material representations. |
| Ethical lending and collections | Apply transparent terms, fair dealing, and accountable collection practices. |
| Investment and equity integrity | Preserve authentic agreements, authorized amendments, accurate capitalization records, and traceable approvals. |
| Market integrity | Maintain controls against deceptive representations, manipulation, and improper trading. |
| Conflicts of interest | Disclose and independently manage material competing interests and related-party relationships. |
| Fiduciary and contractual conduct | Document and comply with applicable fiduciary duties and contractual responsibilities. |
| Intellectual-property integrity | Respect ownership, licensing, confidentiality, and legitimate founder rights. |
| Institutional influence | Disclose material influence over standards bodies, proof producers, verifiers, auditors, and certification authorities. |
| Governance and accountability | Maintain responsible oversight, documented decisions, and effective internal controls. |
| Independent oversight | Permit appropriately scoped independent assessment and credible investigation. |
| Whistleblower protection | Maintain safe reporting, anti-retaliation safeguards, and fair investigations. |
| Remediation and continuing eligibility | Correct substantiated violations, disclose material changes, and undergo periodic reassessment. |

### 7.2 Individual Accountability and Financial Representation

No individual SHOULD evade applicable Trusted Capital requirements by operating independently, acting through an intermediary, or concealing a principal or controlling financial interest.

Institutional certification does not automatically certify every representative, and individual credentials do not establish the trustworthiness of an undisclosed principal.

### 7.3 Proof of Financial Independence

Financial relationships capable of materially influencing a proof producer, verifier, certification authority, standards body, benchmark, registry, or published conclusion SHOULD be disclosed to appropriately authorized independent assessment.

Legitimate confidentiality may be preserved without granting immunity from independent scrutiny.

### 7.4 Ethical Capital Principle

Investors, family offices, and other financial interests participating in recognized Proof Economy activities SHOULD be subject to appropriate requirements of authenticity, independence, integrity, transparency, and accountability.

**Capital may finance the production of proof. It cannot purchase the truth of that proof.**

---

### 8.5 Related-Party Integrity and Material Influence

Integrity risk can propagate through relationships that create material ownership, control, governance, financing, or influence. Recognized participation therefore MUST assess relevant related-party relationships rather than evaluating each participant as though it exists in isolation.

A participant's eligibility MUST consider material relationships with persons or institutions that fail the Trusted Baseline or applicable requirements of this Code. Relevant relationships include, as applicable:

- Beneficial ownership or controlling investment;
- Board membership, governance rights, or appointment authority;
- Material financing, lending, or contractual leverage;
- Control through subsidiaries, affiliates, portfolio companies, investment vehicles, nominees, or intermediaries;
- Authority over standards, certification, verification, registry, benchmark, or proof-related decisions; and
- Other relationships capable of materially influencing conduct or outcomes.

A related party's failure does **not** automatically establish misconduct by another person or entity merely by association. However, where the related party exercises material ownership, control, governance, financing, or influence, the affected participant MUST NOT receive or retain recognized trusted status until the relationship, resulting integrity risk, and any necessary safeguards or remediation have been independently assessed and satisfactorily resolved under documented procedures.

A participant MUST NOT evade an integrity determination through subsidiaries, portfolio companies, intermediaries, nominees, affiliates, investment vehicles, representatives, or other related entities.

Passive or immaterial investment, without meaningful control or influence, SHOULD NOT by itself disqualify an otherwise eligible participant. Assessments MUST be evidence-based, proportionate to the relationship and risk, and provide appropriate notice, an opportunity to respond, and challenge or appeal procedures.

**The integrity assessment follows material influence through the chain; it does not impose guilt by mere association.**

## 9. Core Duties

As a condition of recognized participation or certification, the applicable participant MUST formally accept the version of this Code required by the governing participation terms.

| Duty | Minimum requirement |
|---|---|
| Truthful representation | Accurately state what was tested, verified, certified, funded, or endorsed; disclose material limitations. |
| Evidence integrity | Preserve provenance and records; prohibit fabrication, selective concealment, and unauthorized alteration. |
| Independent judgment | Disclose and manage material financial, personal, and organizational conflicts; recuse when necessary. |
| Ethical business practices | Use fair contracting, truthful negotiations, lawful transactions, and responsible treatment of counterparties. |
| Financial integrity | Maintain appropriate financial records, disclose relevant controlling interests, and prohibit improper influence over proof outcomes. |
| IP and confidentiality | Respect lawful intellectual-property rights, licensing boundaries, confidential information, and authorized access. |
| Individual accountability | Identify responsible decision-makers and representatives to authorized oversight, even when public confidentiality is warranted. |
| Competence and due care | Accept only work within demonstrated capability; use documented methods and correct material errors. |
| Non-retaliation | Protect good-faith complaints, independent review, and lawful whistleblowing. |
| Auditable governance | Maintain records and permit appropriately scoped independent assessments and background checks where lawful and proportionate. |
| Remediation | Cooperate with fair investigations, correct substantiated violations, and accept proportionate sanctions. |

## 10. Adoption, Verification, and Enforcement

An accountable governing body applying this Code MUST identify:

- The applicable Code version;
- The scope of the participant's obligations;
- Objective participation or certification criteria;
- Required evidence;
- The assessor or oversight authority;
- Assessment dates and validity periods;
- Material limitations;
- Complaint and challenge procedures;
- Conflict-based recusals;
- Notice and opportunity to respond;
- Appeal procedures; and
- Corrective-action and remediation processes.

Available responses to substantiated violations MAY include corrective-action plans, enhanced monitoring, suspension or revocation of recognition or certification, termination under applicable agreements, and referrals where legally appropriate.

Certification under this Code is evidence of assessed requirements within a defined scope and period. It is not a declaration that a person or institution is universally ethical.

---

## 11. No Purchased Exemption

No individual or institution may purchase, inherit, conceal, or self-declare immunity from ethical obligations that it has explicitly accepted as a condition of recognized Proof Economy participation.

No financial contributor or governing participant SHOULD control the independent evaluation of its own conduct.

Funding, market position, institutional prestige, technical capability, possession of a credential, or use of a Proof Protocol specification does not itself establish ethical compliance.

---

## 12. Continuous Assurance

Trust is not a permanent credential.

Changes in leadership, ownership, funding, incentives, material relationships, or conduct MAY require reassessment. Periodic review, event-driven review, documented corrective action, and appropriate preservation of records SHOULD form part of continuing assurance.

---

## 13. Relationship to Proof Protocol

This Code is a **foundational governance document**, not a PP-SPEC technical specification.

Proof Protocol specifications define technical requirements for independently verifiable proof. This Code defines ethical and institutional obligations that may govern recognized participation in the Proof Economy.

Technical conformance to a PP-SPEC does not, by itself, establish ethical compliance with this Code. Ethical compliance does not substitute for technical proof validity.

The two layers are intentionally distinct:

> **Technical foundation:** What constitutes proof.  
> **Ethical foundation:** What conduct is required of recognized participants entrusted with producing, verifying, certifying, governing, financing, or materially influencing proof.

---

## 14. Final Questions

**Proof of what? Who produced the proof? Who verified it? Can the producer and verifier be trusted for the stated purpose? Are they authentic, independent, competent, and accountable? Can their methods and conclusions be examined and challenged?**

Before there can be trusted proof, there must be a Trusted Baseline.
